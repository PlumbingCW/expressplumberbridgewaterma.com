Repiping in Bridgewater, MA

When old pipes keep leaking or the water runs brown, patching stops making sense. We replace failing supply lines in copper or PEX and keep the disruption down.

Call (508) 389-4045

When repiping beats another patch

Repiping in Bridgewater, MA replaces the supply lines inside a house when patching no longer keeps up. A house with a few leaks in old pipe, brown water at the tap or pressure that has dropped over the years is a candidate. The repair is planned work, so you can compare options and schedule it.

If a pipe is spraying water now, that is an urgent repair first. Our burst pipe repair page covers it. Repiping is what you discuss once the water is off and you are tired of the pattern.

Signs your pipes are wearing out

  • ✓

    Pinhole leaks that show up in different places over time.

  • ✓

    Brown or orange water, especially after the line has sat unused.

  • ✓

    Reduced pressure at several fixtures, not just one.

  • ✓

    Green corrosion or crusty buildup at joints and fittings.

  • ✓

    A known problem pipe material, such as galvanized steel or polybutylene.

One of these does not mean you need a repipe. A pattern of them does. We look at the pipes and tell you what is failing.

Copper or PEX

MaterialStrengthsTrade-offs
CopperLong track record, tolerates heat, fire resistantHigher material cost and more labor, and aggressive water can corrode it
PEXFlexible, quick to run through walls, resists freezing damage betterNot for outdoor sun exposure, and fittings must be done right
CPVCInexpensive and rigidCan become brittle with age and is less common now

Both copper and PEX are used in Massachusetts homes, and the plumbing code allows several materials. We recommend the one that suits your house and explain why.

What a repipe actually involves

A whole-house repipe replaces the supply lines from the main valve to every fixture. The crew opens small access holes in walls and ceilings, runs new lines and connects the fixtures. A partial repipe replaces one section, such as a bathroom group or a single run in a basement.

We plan the work in stages so you can use water at the end of each day when possible. Patching holes is a separate step, and we tell you what we leave for a drywall contractor. A new main valve and pressure check are part of the job.

Pipes inside the house versus the service line

Repiping covers the pipes inside your house. The service line from the street to your meter or main valve is a different system. If that line is old, replacing it at the same time can make sense. Our water line repair page explains the line, and the town lists a lead service line program that is worth asking about.

Ask the Bridgewater Water Department about your line. We check the material at the main valve and tell you what we see. A galvanized or lead service line is worth replacing when you are already opening walls.

What to do before the crew arrives

  • ✓

    Clear access to the water heater, the main shutoff and any wall or closet the crew must open.

  • ✓

    Move valuables and cover furniture near the work areas.

  • ✓

    Plan for the water to be off for part of the day and fill a few containers.

  • ✓

    Keep children and pets away from open walls, tools and cut pipe.

  • ✓

    Tell us about any older insulation or wrapping on pipes, which should be left alone until it is identified.

Safety comes first on a repipe. We tell you what we find as the walls open and what it means for the plan.

Code and sources for this work

New supply piping must be listed for potable water and installed to 248 CMR 10.00. According to the EPA, the federal Safe Drinking Water Act sets a standard of 0.25 percent lead in wetted plumbing surfaces, which is why modern repipes use lead-free fittings.
