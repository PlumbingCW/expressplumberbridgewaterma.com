When a main line backup needs jetting now

A main line that backs up into the house again and again can need emergency hydro jetting in Bridgewater, MA, and we take the call 24/7. While you wait, stop using water, keep drains closed and keep people and pets out of any flooded basement area. Sewage water carries bacteria, so do not wade in without protection.

Hydro jetting sends water at high pressure through a hose with a nozzle that scours the pipe wall. A cable opens a hole through a clog. A jetter cleans the whole circumference. That is why it works better on grease, scale and roots that return after a snaking visit.

  • ✓

  • ✓

  • ✓

If you are not sure which tool is right, a camera look settles it. Our sewer inspection page explains what it shows.

How a jetting visit works

  1. We locate the cleanout and run a camera when the line condition is unknown.

  2. We choose the nozzle for the clog: a penetrating tip for a blockage, a wide-spray head for scale and grease.

  3. We feed the hose in and work back out, flushing debris toward the main.

  4. We run the camera again where we can and show you the cleaned pipe.

  5. We tell you whether the line needs a repair, and if so, point you to sewer line repair.

What jetting will not fix

Jetting cleans a pipe. It does not repair a crack, a collapse or a sagging section that holds water. High pressure on a broken pipe can make a bad line worse, which is why we check with a camera first and may recommend a cable pass or a repair instead.

Old cast iron may be thin and scaly. Aggressive jetting on fragile pipe can remove scale that was holding the wall together. We use the pressure the pipe can take and explain the risk before we start.

Root intrusion and repeat blockages

Roots find the small gaps at pipe joints and push in. Any lot with trees near the sewer line, from the streets around the Town Common to the outer roads of Stanley and South Bridgewater, can see it, and a main that keeps returning after cabling is the classic sign. A jetter with a cutting head clears the roots, and a root treatment or a spot repair can keep them from coming back.

If roots have split the pipe, jetting is a stopgap. We show you the camera footage so you can see where the line is damaged before you decide.

Code and sources for this work

Jetting is done to the pressure and nozzle guidance in the equipment maker's manual, and the work follows 248 CMR 10.00 for any drain parts we replace. According to the plumbing code, a drain must keep its slope and vent after any cleaning work.
