Garbage Disposal Installation Cost in Bridgewater, MA

The unit is only part of the price. Wiring, the sink flange, the dishwasher connection and the drain fit all change what an install costs.

By the Bridgewater Express Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026.

Call (508) 389-4045

The short answer

A disposal install costs the unit plus labor, and labor changes with what is already under the sink. A swap for a unit of the same size on an existing circuit is the simplest case. A first-time install that needs a new outlet, a switch and a new drain trap is more. We give you a clear price before work starts, and we quote the total, not just the unit.

What an installation visit includes

  1. We turn off the power and remove the old unit or mounting.

  2. We set the new sink flange and mounting assembly.

  3. We connect the drain, the dishwasher line and the power.

  4. We run water and test for leaks and noise.

  5. We show you the reset button and the hex key for clearing a jam.

Septic homes need a different conversation

A disposal adds solids to a septic tank. Some homeowners on septic skip them, and others use one with a larger or more often pumped tank. Ask a septic professional before you add one. If you are unsure whether your house is on town sewer or septic, check your records or ask the town.

A disposal also pushes more material into the drain. If your kitchen line already clogs, see drain cleaning before you add one.

A clear quote lists these items. A number with no detail leaves room for extras on the day.

Repair versus replace on an old disposal

An older disposal that jams often, leaks at the body or runs weakly is near the end. A new unit usually costs less than repeated repairs. A leak at the top flange or a loose connection can often be fixed, and a jam can often be cleared with the hex key.

If the motor hums but the unit will not turn after a reset, the motor or the flywheel may be damaged. Our garbage disposal repair page explains how we decide, and we will tell you when a repair is not worth it.

What drives the price of an install

The unit itself is only part of the bill. A swap with the same mounting and a plug-in cord is quick. A new install with no existing outlet, a new switch or a changed drain layout takes longer and costs more.

A dishwasher connection adds a short step, because the knockout plug inside the disposal has to be removed before the hose is attached. Skipping it leaves the dishwasher unable to drain, a mistake we see after DIY installs.

Ask for the scope in writing, including what happens to the old unit. A clear price covers the unit, the labor and the small parts, so nothing surprising appears when the work is done.

After the install, run cold water for a full minute with the unit on and watch the connections below the sink for drips. Check them again the next day. A slow leak at a new flange or a drain joint is easy to fix in the first week and hard to notice once the cabinet floor is wet.

Sewer or septic: what Bridgewater's permit page says

Bridgewater's permit page asks applicants for Homes and Additions to submit a paid water and sewer receipt from the Water Department if one is available and, if not, an approved septic plan with Board of Health onsite disposal permits. That tells you that both town sewer and septic exist in town.

Before you install a disposal, find out which system your house uses. The page does not say whether every property is on town sewer, and your records or the town can answer that.
