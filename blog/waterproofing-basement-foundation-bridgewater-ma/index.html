Waterproofing a Basement Foundation in Bridgewater, MA

The cheapest basement fix is usually outside. Before you pay for a system, find out where the water enters and why.

By the Bridgewater Express Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026.

Call (508) 389-4045

The short answer

Start outside. Check that the ground slopes away from the house, that gutters are clear and that downspouts discharge several feet from the foundation. Then check the plumbing and the sump. Only then decide on an interior drain or exterior work. Many wet basements are fixed with grading and gutters.

A simple way to find the source

  1. Tape a square of plastic wrap tightly to a wet wall or floor spot, sealing all four edges.

  2. Leave it for a day or two.

  3. If moisture forms on the room side of the plastic, it is condensation from humid air.

  4. If moisture forms behind the plastic, water is coming through the wall or floor.

  5. Check the pipes, the water heater and the sump pit for a plumbing source.

A humid basement needs a dehumidifier and ventilation. A leaking one needs drainage. They are different problems.

A system that treats a symptom does not replace the first three steps. Do those first.

Signs that point to groundwater

  • ✓

    Water enters at the joint where the floor meets the wall.

  • ✓

    Water appears after long wet spells, not just short storms.

  • ✓

    The sump pump runs often or does not keep up.

  • ✓

    White, chalky deposits on the wall, a sign that water has passed through.

  • ✓

    Water rises through the slab in several places.

Those point to a high water table or poor drainage. A drain and a good pump are the usual answer, and sump pump installation explains pits and backups.

What interior drainage can and cannot do

An interior drain collects water at the footing and sends it to a pit, where a pump moves it out. It does not stop water from reaching the foundation, but it controls where the water goes and keeps it off the floor. A wall membrane directs seepage down to the drain.

It does not fix a bowing wall or a foundation that is moving. If you see a horizontal crack or an inward bulge, call a structural engineer. We install drainage and trace plumbing sources, and our basement waterproofing page explains what we do and what we refer out.

Mistakes that waste money

  • ✓

    Paying for an interior system before checking gutters and grading.

  • ✓

    Painting a waterproof coating over a wall that is still seeping.

  • ✓

    Ignoring a sump pump that runs constantly.

  • ✓

    Assuming a crack always means a leak, when many cracks are cosmetic.

  • ✓

    Skipping a backup pump in an area that loses power in storms.

A wall coating hides the symptom and can trap water behind it. Fix the source first.

Grading and downspout basics

Slope of soil near the wallA drop of about 6 inches over the first 10 feetSends water away from the foundation

Gutter conditionClear and tight at every jointOverflow at the corner soaks one spot

These are guidelines, not code, and your lot may need more. They cost little and often fix the problem.

Humidity versus real water

A basement can feel damp without any water coming in. Warm, humid air meets cool walls and floors, and moisture forms on the surface. A dehumidifier set to about 50 percent relative humidity and a closed, ventilated space often solve that, and a cheap hygrometer shows the number.

If a plastic sheet test shows moisture behind the sheet, or you see standing water after rain, you have a real water entry problem. Our basement waterproofing team can tell the difference after a short visit.

How to find where the water comes from

Start outside. Look at how the soil slopes at the foundation, where the downspouts end and whether any gutter overflows at a corner. These simple items cause a large share of wet basements.

Inside, mark the spots where water appears and note when it shows up. Water at the floor and wall joint after heavy rain suggests groundwater pressure, while a damp patch under a window may be a well or a flashing issue.

A written record with photos helps any contractor give an honest answer. If the cause is outside, a cheap fix may solve it, and a plumber will tell you so before suggesting an interior system.

Check the town's current water notices before you test

Bridgewater's water system dashboard reported a Level 1 mild drought status in August 2026 and a mandatory ban on nonessential outdoor water use during a declared supply emergency that took effect in December 2025. Testing a downspout or a grade with a hose counts as outdoor water use.

Check the dashboard for the current status before you run a hose, and watch a real rain instead. A downspout that spills at the corner of the house during a storm shows you what a test would.
