Water Heater Element Repair in Bridgewater, MA

An electric water heater has two heating elements and two thermostats. When one fails, you get lukewarm water or short supply. Here is how to tell which part has gone.

By the Bridgewater Express Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026.

Call (508) 389-4045

The short answer

If the water is lukewarm, the lower element or a thermostat has probably failed. If the water is hot but runs out fast, the lower element is the usual cause, because the upper element heats only the top of the tank. If there is no hot water at all, check the breaker and the reset button first.

Symptoms and the part behind them

SymptomLikely partWhy
No hot water at allBreaker, reset or upper elementThe upper thermostat feeds the whole circuit
Lukewarm waterLower element or thermostatThe lower element does most of the heating
Hot water runs out fastLower elementOnly the top of the tank is being heated
Water too hotStuck thermostatThe thermostat is not cutting power
Breaker trips repeatedlyShorted elementA grounded element draws too much current

A sediment layer can hide the lower element and make it fail early. That is why a tank that is rumbling and warm is often one repair away from a flush.

Safety first

  • ✓

    Turn the breaker off and confirm with a tester that the circuit is dead before you touch a wire.

  • ✓

    Never power an element that is not fully underwater, since it burns out in seconds.

  • ✓

    Close the cold water valve and drain the tank before you remove an element.

  • ✓

    Know where the tank's pressure relief valve discharges and do not block it.

If you are not comfortable working with 240 volt wiring, call a plumber or an electrician. A mistake here is not a small one.

How an element is tested

  1. Turn off power at the breaker and verify it is off.

  2. Remove the access panel and insulation and take a photo of the wiring.

  3. Disconnect the wires from the element.

  4. Set a multimeter to resistance and touch the leads to the element terminals. A reading in the expected range means the element is intact. No reading means it is open.

  5. Touch one lead to a terminal and the other to the metal tank. Any reading means the element has failed to ground.

A replacement element must match the wattage and voltage on the tank's label. If the tank itself leaks, a new element is not the answer, and water heater replacement is.

Why elements fail

Sediment buries the lower element and makes it overheat. Mineral scale coats it and insulates it from the water. An anode rod that has been used up lets the tank and the element corrode faster. Age alone is also a factor, since a tank past ten years often has several worn parts.

A plumber replacing an element also flushes the tank and checks the anode rod and the thermostat, because one failed part is often followed by another. If you are weighing a repair against a new tank, our water heater repair page lays out the trade.

Reading the element's label

What the label showsWhat it tells you
Wattage, for example a common 4,500 watt ratingThe replacement must match or fit the tank's rating
Voltage, usually 240 voltsNever use a 120 volt element on a 240 volt circuit
Screw-in or bolt-in designDecides the wrench or the gasket you need
Low-density or standardLow-density elements are gentler on hard water
Tank label wattage limitA higher wattage element than the tank allows is a fire risk

Take a photo of both labels before you buy a part. A near match is not a match, and the wrong element can damage the tank.

Mistakes that burn out a new element

  • ✓

    Turning the power on before the tank is completely full of water.

  • ✓

    Skipping the flush, which leaves sediment covering the new element.

  • ✓

    Over-tightening or under-tightening the element and leaking at the gasket.

  • ✓

    Reusing an old gasket instead of fitting the new one.

  • ✓

    Setting the thermostat very high, which shortens element life.

After a swap, run a hot tap until water flows steadily before you restore power. That clears air from the tank and protects the element.

When the element is not the real problem

A tank that is leaking, a thermostat that is stuck or a wiring fault can mimic a bad element. A tank that is old enough has thin steel and a spent anode rod, and a new element will not extend that. Several repairs in a short time usually mean the tank is near the end.

If you are weighing a repair against a new tank, ask for the age from the serial number and the condition of the anode rod. We give an honest answer and price both, so you can decide on facts instead of a sales pitch.

Testing an element safely

Switch off the breaker for the heater and confirm with a meter that the power is truly off before you touch a wire. Removing an access panel on a live heater is how people get hurt.

With the power confirmed off, disconnect one wire from the element and test across the terminals with a multimeter. A good element reads a low resistance, while a reading of infinity means the element is open and has failed.

If you are not comfortable working near a 240 volt circuit, this is a job to hand over. A plumber can test both elements and the thermostats in a few minutes and replace what failed.

Licences and permits when you hire help

The Massachusetts consumer fact sheet says plumbing work is done by a licensed master or journeyman plumber, and that minor repairs such as fixing a leaky faucet do not need a permit while replacing piping may. Whether a part swap on a water heater needs a permit in Bridgewater is a question for the Building Department, which runs an electronic permit system.

You can check a plumber's licence with the state Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters by phone or through its online portal. Electrical work on the circuit is a separate license, so tell us if the wiring looks wrong.
