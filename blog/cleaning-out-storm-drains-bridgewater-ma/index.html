Cleaning Out Storm Drains at Your Bridgewater, MA Home

A storm drain that floods the driveway or yard is usually blocked with leaves and silt. Here is how to clear a yard drain safely and what to leave to the town.

By the Bridgewater Express Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026.

Call (508) 389-4045

The short answer

Most storm drain trouble is a blocked grate or a catch basin full of debris. Clear the grate by hand with gloves, remove silt from the basin and flush the line with a hose. If water still backs up, the pipe is blocked or broken and needs a cable or a camera. Never climb into a drain.

Which drain is yours

  • ✓

    A catch basin or area drain in your yard or driveway that serves your own roof and paving is yours.

  • ✓

    A grate at the curb or in the street belongs to the town.

  • ✓

    A basement floor drain is part of the sanitary system, not a storm drain.

  • ✓

    A downspout that connects underground to a pipe is part of your private drainage.

A storm drain and a sanitary sewer are separate systems. Do not pour anything harmful into a storm drain, since it can end up in local water.

How to clear a yard drain

  1. Wear gloves and boots, and watch for traffic if the drain is near the road.

  2. Lift the grate and remove leaves, trash and loose debris by hand or with a trowel.

  3. Scoop silt and sand out of the catch basin and bag it.

  4. Run a garden hose into the basin and watch whether the water leaves.

  5. If it backs up, feed a drain auger or a flexible rod into the outlet pipe.

If you push a cable and meet resistance, stop. A cracked or collapsed pipe can be damaged by force.

Roots, silt and collapsed pipe

A drain that clears and fills again within a rain or two usually has a pipe problem. Roots enter joints, silt collects in a pipe with no slope and old corrugated or clay pipe can crush or shift. A camera shows what the line looks like, and our sewer inspection page describes how that works.

If the pipe needs repair, we dig or reline it as the line allows. A drain that discharges into a dry well or a lawn can also saturate, which is a design problem to fix, not a clog.

When to call a plumber

Call if a drain backs up after you clear it, if water is entering the house from a downspout line or if you cannot find where the line goes. We clear yard drains and catch basins that serve a house, and we use the same cables and jetting we use on sewer lines. Hydro jetting is useful for a line that fills with silt and roots.

A street grate that is blocked is the town's to clear. Report it to the town instead of digging in the road.

Safety rules for working around drains

  • ✓

    Wear heavy gloves, since leaves hide glass, needles and sharp debris.

  • ✓

    Never reach into a pipe with a bare hand.

  • ✓

    Never enter a catch basin or a manhole, because gases and a lack of oxygen can be deadly.

  • ✓

    Keep clear of traffic if the drain is near a road.

  • ✓

    Put silt in a bag and do not rinse it into the street.

If a basin is deep or the pipe looks unstable, stop and call. The risks are real, and a plumber has the equipment to work safely.

Knowing where your water goes tells you where to look when a spot floods. Trace each downspout to its end.

Seasonal habits that prevent flooding

Clear grates after leaf fall and before spring melt. In a heavy rain forecast, walk the property and check that the drains are open. A few minutes with a glove and a bucket is far easier than clearing a basin that has packed with silt.

If a yard drain floods in the same spot every storm, the problem is design, not debris. A pipe may be too small, may lack slope or may discharge into soil that cannot take the water. A plumber can inspect the line and suggest a fix.

Knowing what belongs to you and what belongs to the town

A drain on your lot, a downspout line and a dry well are usually the owner's responsibility. A catch basin in the street belongs to the town, and the Highway Department handles those.

If a street basin is blocked and floods the road, report it to the Highway Department at 151 High Street instead of clearing it yourself. Working in the road is unsafe, and the town has the equipment and the authority to do it.

If water backs up from your own pipe and the line leaves your property, a camera inspection shows where the problem sits. That tells you who needs to fix it and saves an argument later.

Who handles street drains in Bridgewater

Bridgewater's website lists the Highway Department at 151 High Street among its offices and says the Municipal Office Building is at 66 Central Square. For a blocked street grate or a road flooding issue, contact the town through those offices and describe the location.

Keep your own yard drains clear so that your water does not become the street's problem. If you are unsure whether a drain is yours or the town's, ask before you start work.
