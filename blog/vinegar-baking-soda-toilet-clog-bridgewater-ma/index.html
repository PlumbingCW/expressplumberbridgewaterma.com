Vinegar and Baking Soda for a Clogged Toilet: A Bridgewater Guide

The kitchen-cabinet trick is popular, but it rarely clears a solid clog. Here is what it can do, what works better and when to stop.

By the Bridgewater Express Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026.

Call (508) 389-4045

The short answer

Vinegar and baking soda can loosen a light, soft clog such as an excess of toilet paper. It will not clear a toy, a wad of wipes or a solid blockage in the trap. It makes carbon dioxide foam that stirs the water, but the force is small and the clog usually stays put.

How to try it safely

  1. Stop the tank from refilling by closing the valve behind the toilet if the bowl is high.

  2. Remove some water from the bowl with a cup if it is close to the rim.

  3. Pour a cup of baking soda into the bowl, then slowly add two cups of vinegar.

  4. Let it foam and sit for 20 to 30 minutes.

  5. Pour a bucket of hot tap water, not boiling, from waist height to push it through.

Boiling water can crack a porcelain bowl. Never mix this with a chemical drain cleaner, because the combination can release harmful fumes.

What works better

  • ✓

    A flange plunger, which has a rubber extension that seals the toilet outlet.

  • ✓

    A closet auger, a short cable with a curved tip made for toilets.

  • ✓

    A bucket of hot water with a squirt of dish soap to lubricate a paper clog.

  • ✓

    Patience, since a pushed clog may need a few minutes to settle.

Use a plunger with firm, steady strokes and keep the seal. Most toilet clogs clear within a few tries.

When the clog is not in the toilet

If the toilet clears and the tub gurgles, or other drains are slow, the blockage is past the toilet in the branch or the main. A plunger will not reach it and neither will a kitchen remedy. That is a job for a cable or a camera, covered on our drain cleaning page.

If the toilet backs up and sewage appears in a floor drain, stop using water in the house and call. A main line problem is a bigger issue, and sewer line cleaning explains how it is handled.

Why toilets clog repeatedly

A toilet that clogs more than once a month has a cause. It may be a partial blockage in the line, a low-flow bowl that is too weak for the amount of paper, a flapper that does not release enough water or a vent problem that slows the flush. Wipes labeled flushable do not break down like paper and collect on any rough spot in the pipe.

If the toilet is old, a new model with a good flush design is often cheaper than repeated clearing.

Matching the cause to the tool matters more than the product you choose. A solid object will not dissolve, and a vent problem will not clear with foam.

Mistakes that turn a clog into a call-out

  • ✓

    Flushing again and again, which fills the bowl and floods the floor.

  • ✓

    Pouring a chemical drain cleaner into a bowl that is already full.

  • ✓

    Using a sink plunger, which cannot seal a toilet outlet.

  • ✓

    Forcing a metal snake that scratches the porcelain.

  • ✓

    Ignoring a bowl that rises in a tub or floor drain, which signals a line problem.

Stopping early is a skill. If two good attempts with a flange plunger fail, the clog is not where you are pushing.

What a plumber does differently

A plumber starts by asking which other fixtures are slow, because that locates the blockage. A closet auger reaches the trap. If that fails, the toilet comes off the floor so the outlet and the flange can be checked directly, and a cable can reach the line.

Resetting the toilet needs a new wax ring and a level seat. That is part of the job when we pull one, and it is why a toilet that rocks or leaks at the base after a DIY removal needs attention, as covered under toilet repair.

How to plunge a toilet properly

Use a flange plunger, the kind with a rubber lip that folds out. Make sure the bowl has enough water to cover the cup, then seat the plunger over the outlet and push slowly at first to push out the air.

Pump with steady strokes for about twenty seconds, then pull sharply to break the seal. The pulling stroke often does more work than the pushing one. Repeat a few rounds before you decide it has failed.

If the water level drops and the bowl clears, flush once to confirm. If it does not move after several honest tries, stop and call, because a harder push only risks a spill or a cracked bowl.

Iron stains and Bridgewater's water

Bridgewater's water dashboard says brownish discoloration is usually caused by naturally occurring minerals, mainly iron, stirred up when flow changes during hydrant flushing or main work. It says the water is generally not harmful and that running cold water for a few minutes should clear it.

A rust stain in a bowl is cosmetic and does not cause clogs. If the discoloration persists, the dashboard directs you to contact the Water Department. A toilet that keeps staining despite clear water may have a worn tank part we can check.
