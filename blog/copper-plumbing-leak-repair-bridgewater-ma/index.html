Copper Pipe Leak Repair in Bridgewater, MA

Copper pipe lasts a long time, but joints fail and pinholes form. Here is how to tell which you have and what a lasting repair involves.

By the Bridgewater Express Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026.

Call (508) 389-4045

The short answer

A copper leak is either at a joint or through the pipe wall. A joint leak is usually a failed solder or fitting and is a clean repair. A pinhole in the pipe wall is a sign of corrosion, and several pinholes in one system mean the pipe is wearing out.

Where copper leaks come from

  • ✓

    Failed solder joints, often at fittings where heat or movement stressed them.

  • ✓

    Pinholes from corrosion, which can come from water chemistry, flux residue or erosion from fast flow.

  • ✓

    Freeze damage, where ice splits the pipe lengthwise.

  • ✓

    Physical damage from a nail, a hammer blow or a remodel.

  • ✓

    Dissimilar metal connections where copper meets steel without a proper fitting.

Knowing which one you have decides the repair. A joint can be reworked. A pinhole is often the first of many.

What to do right now

  1. Close the main water valve and open the lowest tap to drain the lines.

  2. Dry the area with a towel, since wet pipe cannot be soldered.

  3. Wrap the leak with a rubber patch and a clamp, or use a push-fit repair coupling as a temporary measure.

  4. Turn the water on slowly and watch the repair.

  5. Call a plumber for the permanent fix.

A temporary clamp is not a permanent repair. If you cannot stop the leak, keep the main off and call. For a spraying line, see burst pipe repair.

How a lasting repair is done

A plumber cuts out the damaged section, cleans the ends, fits new pipe with couplings and either solders or presses the joints. Press fittings use a tool that crimps a fitting with a seal, and they work well in wet conditions where soldering is hard. For a long run of pinholes, replacing the section or repiping the system is the real answer.

A plumber also looks for the cause. If a high water pressure or a failing pressure reducing valve is stressing the pipe, a new section will fail too.

Pinholes, water chemistry and when to stop patching

Pinhole leaks often come from the inside of the pipe. Water chemistry, flow speed, flux left from soldering and certain minerals can eat through copper over decades. Once pinholes begin, they tend to continue.

Count the leaks. One in 25 years is a repair. Three in two years is a pattern. A plumber can cut a section open and show you the inside of the pipe, which usually makes the decision clear.

All of these are valid when used in the right place. The mistake is choosing a method for convenience instead of for the failure.

Mistakes that lead to a second leak

  • ✓

    Soldering a pipe that still has water in it, which leaves a weak joint.

  • ✓

    Using an unlisted fitting for a pressurized supply.

  • ✓

    Skipping the pressure test before closing the wall.

  • ✓

    Ignoring the cause, such as high pressure or a failing reducing valve.

  • ✓

    Joining copper to galvanized steel without a dielectric fitting.

A repair that is tested at full pressure before the wall closes catches most of these.

Checking pressure before and after a repair

Household supply pressure is commonly in the range of 40 to 80 pounds per square inch. Pressure well above that stresses every joint and fixture in the house. A simple gauge on an outdoor hose bib shows the number in seconds.

If the number is high, a pressure reducing valve near the main shutoff may be failing. Replacing it is a small job compared with repeated leaks. We check pressure on every copper repair and tell you the reading.

Reading the signs of a failing copper line

Green or white crust on a pipe or fitting is a sign that water has been seeping there. A small pinhole may show only as a damp spot or a faint stain on the ceiling below, and it may spray a fine mist when the pressure is up.

Pinholes in several places along one run point to the pipe itself wearing out, often from aggressive water, high velocity or an old installation. Fixing only the one that leaks today means chasing the next one next month.

A plumber looks at the age of the pipe, the pattern of leaks and the water pressure before suggesting a repair or a replacement. That keeps the fix matched to the real cause.

Permits and licences in Massachusetts

The Massachusetts plumbers and gas fitters consumer fact sheet says plumbing work must be done by a licensed master or journeyman plumber and that a local plumbing inspector issues a permit before plumbing is installed, altered, removed, replaced or repaired. It treats minor repairs such as fixing a leaky faucet or clearing a blocked drain as not needing a permit, but that exception does not cover replacing piping.

Replacing a section of copper pipe is piping work, so ask the Bridgewater Building Department how it handles it, and ask your plumber to show you the permit and license.
