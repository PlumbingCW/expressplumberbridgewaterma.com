Cost of Sewer Line Repair in Bridgewater, MA

Two sewer repairs on the same street can differ a lot in cost. The method, the length, the depth and what sits on top of the pipe explain most of the gap.

By the Bridgewater Express Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026.

Call (508) 389-4045

The short answer

A short spot repair costs a fraction of a full replacement. A line that needs to be dug up under a driveway, a patio or a mature tree costs more than one in an open lawn. We do not quote a flat number without seeing the line. A camera inspection tells us what is needed, and then we give you a clear price before work starts.

Spot repair, lining or replacement

A spot repair cuts out one failed section and replaces it. It suits a single crack or offset and leaves the rest of the old pipe. Lining inserts a resin sleeve that hardens inside the pipe. It suits a pipe that is intact but cracked or rooted along its length and cannot fix a sagging pipe.

Full replacement removes the old pipe and installs new. It suits a collapsed or badly sagged line. Trenchless replacement methods reduce digging but need the right conditions. Our sewer line repair page lays out the trade-offs in a table.

Does insurance cover it

Homeowners policies often exclude sewer line repair, and many treat the line as wear. A water backup endorsement may cover cleanup after a backup but not the pipe. Some utilities and insurers sell a separate service line plan. Read your policy before you need it, and ask your agent specific questions.

If a tree on your property split the line, ask whether your policy covers it. Do not count on it.

A contractor who answers these clearly is easier to compare. Vague answers mean a quote that can change.

Buying a house with an unknown sewer line

A sewer repair can be a large expense, and it often appears after you move in. A camera run before closing costs far less than a surprise afterward. If the footage shows a defect, you can negotiate the price, ask the seller to repair it or plan the repair with a clear number.

Our sewer inspection gives you a recording and a plain explanation. Ask for it early in the purchase so you have time to act on what it shows.

Why two quotes can look so different

A low number may assume the best case, such as a short dig in open lawn, while a high number may assume a long run, hard paving or a deep line. The footage and the dig plan explain the gap, so ask for both.

Access changes cost more than most people expect. A line under a driveway, a patio or a mature tree costs more to reach, and restoration of that surface is a separate job that someone has to pay for.

Compare the scope, not only the total. The better quote names the method, the length, the restoration and the warranty, and it tells you what happens if the pipe is worse than the camera showed.

Ask whether the town needs a permit and an inspection for the work, and who arranges them. In Bridgewater the Building Department handles plumbing permits, and a sewer repair that is not inspected can cause trouble at a later sale. A contractor who knows the process and handles it for you is worth more than a small saving on the quote.

Town sewer, septic and permits in Bridgewater

Bridgewater's permit page asks for a paid water and sewer receipt from the Water Department on certain permits, and an approved septic plan when none is available, and links a Sewer Division. Its Building Department says a permit card must be in hand before work begins.

A sewer repair is regulated work, and the connection to a public main may involve the town. Ask the Building Department and the Sewer Division what they need before any dig, and let a licensed plumber handle the permit.
