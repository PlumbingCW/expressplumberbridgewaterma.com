Planning a water heater install in Bridgewater

Water heater installation in Bridgewater, MA is a planned job when the old unit still works, you are changing fuel or moving the tank, or a renovation gives you a chance to upgrade. Planning ahead lets you compare units and schedule a time that suits you.

If your tank has already failed and water is on the floor, that is an urgent swap. Our emergency replacement service covers that. Both jobs end with a new unit that is vented, connected and tested, but a planned install gives us room to adjust the venting and gas or power supply first.

Sizing the unit

  • ✓

    Count the people in the house and the bathrooms. A larger household needs a bigger tank or a higher recovery rate.

  • ✓

    Check the space. Many basements and closets have height limits that rule out a taller tank.

  • ✓

    Note the fuel. Switching gas to electric, or the reverse, changes the work.

  • ✓

    Think about peak use. Running a shower, a dishwasher and a washer together drains a small tank quickly.

Heat pump water heaters are also an option for electric replacements, and they need open air clearance and a drain for condensate. We tell you if your space suits one.

Venting, gas and expansion tanks

Gas water heaters need a vent path that carries exhaust outside, and the vent has to slope, seal and clear combustibles as the manufacturer and code require. A stainless or approved vent liner is often needed when a new, more efficient unit replaces an older one. Poor venting is a safety risk, so we check it before we light a new unit.

An expansion tank absorbs the pressure rise when the water heats. Many homes on a closed system need one, and a missing or waterlogged tank is a common reason a relief valve drips. We test the tank pressure and add one when the system calls for it.

From quote to finished install

  1. We ask about the unit now, the fuel, the space and your hot water use.

  2. We give you a clear price before work starts and a time slot.

  3. We set the unit, run new supply lines and shutoffs, and connect gas or power.

  4. We vent, test for leaks, set the temperature and walk you through the controls.

  5. We handle the permit step with the town when the job calls for it.

Permit and code in Bridgewater

Massachusetts plumbing work falls under 248 CMR 10, the Uniform State Plumbing Code, and a licensed plumber pulls the permit from the local plumbing inspector. Bridgewater's Building Department runs an electronic permit system and its page notes that a permit card must be in hand before work begins. Permit steps vary by job, so ask the department about yours.

Check any plumber's licence through the state's online verification before you sign. A plumber who asks you to skip the permit is a reason to find a different plumber.

Code and sources for this work

According to the state code, a new install must meet 248 CMR 10.00 for piping, a temperature and pressure relief valve and a safe discharge. Gas work follows 248 CMR 5.00, and the local inspector signs off under a permit.
