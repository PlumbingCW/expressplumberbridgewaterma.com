Scotland the village and Scotland Boulevard the corridor

The state historic survey describes Scotland as a farming settlement from the Federal period, where Scottish natives settled in the early 1700s. A Trinitarian church built in 1821 and a meetinghouse from 1823 survive. Modest sidehall cottages were added later in the 1800s.

Scotland Boulevard is a different matter. The 2002 master plan treats Elm Street and Scotland Boulevard as one land use district, and the boulevard is lined with commercial and industrial buildings. This page covers the residential village area, and our commercial plumbing page covers the buildings along the boulevard.

Sewer or septic, and why it matters here

The town's 2002 master plan stated that on-site septic systems served most homes in Bridgewater and that fewer than ten percent of new homes since 1990 had connected to municipal sewer. That plan is more than twenty years old, so treat it as background.

The question to answer today is simple. Does your house connect to a town sewer, or does it have a tank and leach field? The answer changes who you call for a backup and which inspections apply when you sell.

How to tell what your house has

  • ✓

    Look for a sewer bill from the town. A property on septic does not receive one.

  • ✓

    Look in the yard for a round or square lid, which is often a tank access cover.

  • ✓

    Check the closing documents for a Title 5 inspection report.

  • ✓

    Ask the Health Department at 66 Central Square, which handles septic reports.

  • ✓

    Ask the Water and Sewer office whether your street has a public sewer main.

If you find a tank, keep vehicles and heavy loads off it, and write down where it sits. A drain backup in a house with a septic tank can start at the tank, as our drain cleaning page explains.

What the town says about septic inspections

According to the town's Health Department page, a $20 fee applies to any Title 5 inspection report that is a conditional pass or a failure, and passing reports carry no fee. The page also lists septic installers who work in the area.

We do not perform septic system installation. We help with the plumbing inside the house, the line that leaves it and the question of whether a backup is a house problem or a tank problem.

Finding the cleanout before you need it

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives a plumber direct access to the house drain. In many houses it sits in the basement near the main stack or just outside the foundation wall. Finding it on a calm day saves time when a drain stops.

Do not remove the cap if sewage is backing up, because pressure behind it can release suddenly. Tell us where it is, and we will explain what to do next.
