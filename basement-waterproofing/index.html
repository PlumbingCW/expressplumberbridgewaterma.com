Finding where the water comes from

Basement waterproofing in Bridgewater, MA starts with one question. Where does the water come from? A leaking pipe, a failed sump pump, a cracked wall, groundwater rising through the floor and a bad grade outside all look similar and need different fixes. We check the plumbing first, then the pump, then the walls and floor.

If water is sudden and heavy, that is an emergency. Our emergency sump pump service is open 24/7. This page covers the planned side, where you have time to find the cause and fix it right.

Common sources of a wet basement

  • ✓

    Groundwater rising through the slab or the joint where the floor meets the wall.

  • ✓

    Cracks or leaking tie holes in a poured or block wall.

  • ✓

    Gutters and downspouts that dump water at the foundation.

  • ✓

    Soil that slopes toward the house.

  • ✓

    A plumbing leak, such as a water heater, a supply line or a drain.

Some causes are not plumbing at all. Gutters and grading are cheap to fix and should be done before an expensive system. We tell you when the answer is outside our work.

Why this part of Massachusetts sees groundwater

Bridgewater sits along the Taunton River, with Lake Nippenicket and part of the Hockomock Swamp Wildlife Management Area in the western part of town. Low ground near water means a higher water table in wet seasons, and a basement below that level can take water through the floor even when walls are sound.

That is why interior drainage and a sump pit are common answers in this region. They collect water at the footing and pump it away. They do not stop water from reaching the foundation, but they control where it goes.

Interior drainage and exterior waterproofing

ApproachHow it worksTrade-off
Interior drain and sumpCollects water at the footing and pumps it outManages water but does not keep it out of the wall
Wall membraneDirects wall seepage to the drainHides the wall, so cracks cannot be watched
Exterior waterproofingSeals and drains the outside of the wallRequires digging and costs the most
Grading, gutters and downspoutsMoves roof and surface water awayInexpensive, but may not stop rising groundwater

A sump system is only as good as its pump and backup. See our sump pump installation page for pit and backup details.

What we do and what we will refer out

We install interior drains, sump pits and pumps, and we trace and repair plumbing sources. We do not do foundation structural repair or exterior excavation of a full wall. If a crack is moving or a wall is bowing, a structural engineer or a foundation contractor is the right call, and we say so.

Humidity can feel like a leak. A dehumidifier and a vapor barrier may be the right answer for a damp but not wet basement. We tell you honestly when a system is more than you need.

Code and sources for this work

Interior drainage and sump systems must discharge where the town and the plumbing code standard allow, and 248 CMR 10.00 limits connections to the sanitary sewer. The EPA guidance on indoor moisture says to fix the water source before any cosmetic coating.
