Halifax is built around its ponds

East and West Monponsett Ponds lie near the center of Halifax, separated in places by a strip of land about 150 feet wide, and Route 58 crosses that strip. Halifax covers 17.3 square miles and about 1.2 of them are water. A town that wet has plenty of low ground, and low ground means groundwater work.

For homeowners, that shows up as damp cellars, sump pumps that run all spring and service lines that run through soft, wet soil. Our emergency plumber dispatch reaches Halifax around the clock for all of it.

Wet-ground signs that point to plumbing or to groundwater

  • ✓

    A pump that cycles every few minutes in dry weather may signal a leak into the pit, not groundwater.

  • ✓

    A soft patch in the yard between the street and the house can be a service line leak.

  • ✓

    A water bill that jumped with no change in use is worth a meter test.

  • ✓

    A damp wall after a heavy rain is usually groundwater, not a pipe.

We start with the plumbing, then the pit. Leak detection covers the meter test, and water line repair covers the buried line.

Halifax water in plain terms

The state's source water assessment for the Halifax Water Department lists three gravel-packed wells at Richmond Park and the YMCA site. Water from the Richmond Park wells is treated with potassium permanganate to remove iron and manganese, along with a corrosion control chemical and chlorine. The YMCA well gets corrosion control and chlorine.

That mix means iron and manganese are a design concern here, as in the neighboring towns. If you see rust-colored or dark water, run the cold tap for a few minutes and check an aerator. If it persists, call the department. We handle the pipes and tank.

Winter and spring checks for a Halifax house

Dispatch to Halifax is open around the clock, and these checks are for the weeks before something fails.

Find your shutoffs

Locate the main water valve and each fixture stop now, and turn each once so it does not seize.

Insulate exposed pipe

Wrap pipes in unheated spaces and keep a cabinet door open on an exterior wall on a freezing night.

Test the pump

Pour a bucket of water into the sump pit and watch the float lift and the pump start.

Roads, rail and neighbors

Routes 58 and 106 are the two main roads and meet just east of the town center. Route 36 ends at Route 106 and Route 105 starts about 1.5 miles to the west. Halifax has its own station on the MBTA Kingston commuter line. It borders Hanson, Pembroke, Plympton, Middleborough, Bridgewater and East Bridgewater.

Bridgewater borders Halifax on the west, so the two towns are close. We also take calls from nearby Plympton. The population was 7,749 in 2020, and the town is mostly residential, so most calls are homes.
