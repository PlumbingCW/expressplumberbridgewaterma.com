Commercial leak detection

A leak in a commercial building can hide above a drop ceiling, inside a chase or under a slab. We use the same pressure tests, listening gear and thermal imaging we use in houses, scaled to the building. A property manager benefits from a written note of where the leak is and what it will take to fix.

If a tenant reports a leak, tell us who owns which part of the system. Our leak detection page explains the methods.

Grease traps, floor drains and backflow

Restaurants and food shops depend on grease traps and interceptors. Pumping and cleaning them is often done by licensed haulers and is governed by local rules, so ask the town's sewer or health department what applies to you. We handle the plumbing side, including the kitchen drain lines and the trap connections.

Backflow preventers protect the public water supply from contamination. Ask your water department whether your device needs testing and how often. We can help you understand what your building has and what the department will ask for.

For landlords and property managers

If you manage several units, tell us at the start. We ask who authorizes the work, how to reach the tenant and whether access is limited to certain hours. For work that is not an emergency, we schedule it so it does not interrupt other tenants.

Keep records of what was done and when. A written description of each visit helps with insurance, lease questions and capital planning.

Code and sources for this work

Commercial work follows 248 CMR 10.00, including rules on fixture counts, floor drains, grease interceptors and backflow protection. According to state drinking water rules, 310 CMR 22.00, cross-connections need protection.
