Sewer backup: what to do first

Sewage coming up through a floor drain, tub or toilet is a sewer line emergency in Bridgewater, MA, and our line is open 24/7 for it. Stop using water, do not flush, and keep people and pets out of the wet area. Do not run the washing machine or dishwasher, because that water will go to the same place.

Wear gloves and boots if you must go in. Sewage carries bacteria and is not something to mop up with bare hands. Then call us. We ask which fixtures are backing up, whether the house is on town sewer or septic and when it last cleared, and we ask about timing.

Signs the line is damaged, not just blocked

  • ✓

    Backups that return within weeks of a cleaning.

  • ✓

    A camera or cable that stops at the same point each time.

  • ✓

    A sunken or soggy patch of lawn along the line from the house to the street.

  • ✓

    Sewer odor in the yard or basement, gurgling in distant fixtures or slow drains throughout the house.

  • ✓

    Fine roots or pieces of clay or cast iron coming back on the cable.

A clog responds to cleaning. A damaged pipe does not, and that is when a repair is the honest answer. If cleaning is all you need, see sewer line cleaning.

Finding the break

We run a camera through the cleanout or a pulled toilet, watch the line on a monitor and locate the problem from above ground with a transmitter in the camera head. That tells us the distance, the depth and the type of damage, whether a crack, an offset joint, a root mass, a belly that holds water or a full collapse.

You can watch the footage with us and get a plain explanation of what is wrong. Our sewer inspection page covers the camera step on its own.

Repair options and the honest trade-offs

OptionFits whenTrade-off
Spot repairOne short section is cracked or offsetNeeds a dig at that spot, and the rest of the old pipe stays
Trenchless liningThe pipe is intact but cracked or rooted along its lengthReduces pipe diameter slightly and cannot fix a collapsed or sagging pipe
Pipe bursting or replacementThe line is collapsed, badly sagged or made of failing materialMore disruption and cost, and the longest result
Cleaning onlyThe pipe is sound and the blockage is the problemDoes not repair damage and may need repeating

Trenchless methods cut down on lawn and driveway digging, but not every line qualifies, and we explain why when it does not.

Roots, old materials and Bridgewater's older pipes

Roots enter at joints, and older clay and cast iron lines have more joints and more scale. Houses on the oldest streets are likelier to have those materials than houses built in the last few decades, but a camera is the only way to know what your line actually is.

If your house is a candidate for a lined or replaced lateral, the camera footage helps you decide. A sewer lateral replacement is also worth considering when you buy a house with an unknown line.

Permits and town sewer in Bridgewater

A repair or replacement of a sewer service often needs a permit from the town, and the plumbing is licensed work in Massachusetts. Bridgewater's Building Department asks for a paid water and sewer receipt on certain permits and an approved septic plan when none exists, so confirm whether you are on town sewer or septic before any dig.

We tell you what the job needs, handle the permit step and call for any inspections. Ask the Building Department how it handles a sewer repair at your address.

Code and sources for this work

Sewer repairs follow 248 CMR 10.00, the state standard for pipe material, slope and cleanouts. Where a property is on septic, state Title 5, 310 CMR 15.00, governs the system, so the permit path depends on which type you have.
