A town this big needs a dispatch plan

Middleborough covers 72.2 square miles, which makes it the second-largest municipality in Massachusetts by area after Plymouth. It has 24,245 residents as of the 2020 census. Middleborough Center is the main village and South Middleboro sits farther down Route 28, so the distance from our Bridgewater base to your door can vary a lot.

When you call, give us the street and the nearest cross street. We pick the route by Route 18, Route 28, Route 44, Route 105 or Interstate 495 depending on where you are, and we plan the visit from there. We never promise an arrival time. We do dispatch around the clock.

What the town's water is treated for

The state's source water assessment for the Middleborough Water Supply lists eleven wells in seven protection areas. All wells receive potassium hydroxide for corrosion control and sodium hypochlorite for disinfection, and five wells are treated to remove iron.

Corrosion control matters at your house because it affects the copper and solder in your pipes over the years. A tank or boiler that has run in treated water for a long time shows scale and sediment on drain-down. We read what comes out and tell you what it means.

No heat or a burst line in Middleborough

Winters bring boiler and frozen pipe calls. Dispatch to Middleborough is available around the clock, and these steps help in the meantime.

Check the boiler basics

Look at the thermostat, the breaker and the pressure gauge. A reading near zero means water was lost.

Smell gas, leave

Leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 from outside before you call us.

Frozen pipe

Open the faucet, warm the area with a hair dryer and never use a flame. If you see a split, close the main.

Burst pipe

Close the main valve, open the lowest tap and switch off power to any wet room.

Cranberries, ponds and the rotary

Middleborough calls itself the Cranberry Capital of the World, and about 1,400 acres were in cranberries in 2015, roughly 3 percent of the nation's cranberry bog acreage. Routes 18, 28 and 44 meet at a two-lane rotary next to Interstate 495, just west of the town center. Ponds along the Lakeville line include Assawompset and Great Quittacas.

Low, wet ground near bogs and ponds is where basements take on groundwater. If you have water in the cellar and a pit with no backup, see sump pump repair. We also take calls from nearby Lakeville and Carver.
