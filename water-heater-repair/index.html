No hot water: what to check first

No hot water is an emergency water heater repair call in Bridgewater, MA, and we pick up 24/7. Before you call, check the basics. On an electric tank, look at the breaker and the reset button on the upper thermostat. On a gas tank, check that the gas valve is on and the pilot or igniter is working.

If you see water at the base of the tank, shut the cold water valve on top and turn the unit off. A leak from a pipe fitting or the relief valve line is repairable. A leak from the tank body is not, and that is when water heater replacement is the honest answer.

Symptoms and the part behind them

Rumbling or poppingSediment cooking under the burner or elementFlush now, replace if the tank is old

A T&P relief valve that drips often is a clue that system pressure is too high. We check the pressure before swapping the valve, since a new valve will drip too if the pressure is the cause.

Gas and electric tanks fail in different ways

Gas units depend on a clean flame, a working thermocouple and a safe vent. Soot, a flickering flame or a smell of gas means we stop and fix the cause before anything else. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first, then call us.

Electric tanks fail at the heating element and thermostat. Upper and lower elements run in sequence, so a dead lower element gives you a short supply of hot water that runs out fast. Replacing an element is quick and inexpensive compared to a tank.

Sediment, iron and anode rods

The state's source water assessment lists iron treatment at the Bridgewater Water Department's wells. Iron and minerals that pass through treatment settle in a tank, and a layer of it insulates the water from the burner or element. That is why an older tank can rumble and take longer to heat.

A yearly flush removes loose sediment. The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes in place of the tank steel, and checking it every few years is the cheapest way to extend tank life. If the rod is gone, the tank is next.

Repair or replace

  • ✓

    Repair when the tank is under about 10 years old, dry on the outside and one part has failed.

  • ✓

    Replace when the tank body leaks, the rust is heavy or repairs are stacking up.

  • ✓

    Consider a tankless unit only when you are ready to change venting and gas or power capacity.

  • ✓

    Ask for the age from the serial number on the label if you do not know it.

A good plumber tells you when not to repair. If we say a part will not solve the problem, we will tell you and price the replacement instead.

Code and sources for this work

According to the Consumer Product Safety Commission, a water heater thermostat should be set no higher than 120 degrees Fahrenheit to reduce scald risk. Repairs to gas controls fall under the Massachusetts fuel gas code, 248 CMR 5.00.
