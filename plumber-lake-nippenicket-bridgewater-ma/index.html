Plumber in the Lake Nippenicket Area, Bridgewater, MA

Lake Nippenicket is a 220-acre pond on the edge of the Hockomock Swamp, with homes along the shoreline roads. Wet ground, wells, septic tanks and sump pumps are the questions that come up most for homes close to water.

The lake and the land around it

According to MassWildlife, Lake Nippenicket is a 220-acre great pond that borders the Hockomock Swamp and feeds the Town River. It has 4.8 miles of shoreline, and much of that shore lies inside the Hockomock Swamp Wildlife Management Area. State information says that limits development mostly to the southern half.

The town's 2002 master plan set up a Lake Nippenicket Residential District and noted Lakeside Drive and Elm Street within the Hockomock Swamp Area of Critical Environmental Concern. The town manages a paved boat ramp off Lakeside Drive.

Why a lakeside address raises different questions

Nothing in the public record says homes here have more plumbing problems than anywhere else. What the record does say is that the land borders a lake and a large wetland. Those two facts shape the questions worth asking, in the same way for any home near water.

Ask about groundwater, about where the sump discharges and about the septic system if you have one. Ask the town before you dig or discharge anything outdoors, because work near wetlands can fall under the Conservation Commission. If you cannot tell whether basement water is groundwater or a plumbing leak, leak detection can separate the two.

Questions for a home near the water

QuestionWhy it matters
Does the basement have a sump pit and pump?A pump needs a working float, a check valve and a clear discharge
Is there a battery backup for outages?A storm that cuts power also stops a single pump
Where does the discharge pipe end?It should not point at a neighbor, a septic area or a foundation
Is the house on septic or sewer?A high water table can slow a leach field
Is the house on a private well or town water?Treatment and testing needs differ

These are checks, not predictions. They are worth doing once, in dry weather, so you know the answers before a storm.

Before the next big rain

  1. Lift the float in the sump pit and confirm the pump starts and the water leaves.

  2. Clear leaves from gutters and confirm downspouts discharge well away from the house.

  3. Walk the discharge pipe outdoors and check that it is open and pointed away from the foundation.

  4. Test any battery backup and note the age of the battery.

  5. Move stored items off the basement floor.

Our sump pump installation and repair page explains pumps, floats and backups in more detail, and basement waterproofing covers where basement water comes from.

If your house has a private well

A private well usually has a pump and a pressure tank, and the plumbing inside the house depends on both. Low pressure, sputtering taps or a pump that cycles often are worth a call before the pump burns out.

Water testing for a private well is separate from plumbing, and the Health Department can point you to the right place. We can connect and service the plumbing side, including shutoffs, filters and softener hookups.

Septic, wells and wetland rules

The Bridgewater Health Department handles Title 5 septic inspection reports and lists septic installers. Questions about work near the lake shore or the swamp belong with the town's Conservation Commission before any digging starts. The Building Department handles plumbing permits for work inside the house.
