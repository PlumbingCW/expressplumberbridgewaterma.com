What Central Square means in Bridgewater

Central Square is the historic center of Bridgewater, at the point where Main Street and South Street meet. The Massachusetts Historical Commission survey of the town describes a residential district that grew up around the square in the middle and late 1800s, with Queen Anne and Colonial Revival houses built around the State College, now Bridgewater State University.

The town's 2002 master plan says a historic district of about 96 structures was created here in 1986 and lists it on the National Register of Historic Places. Boundaries for plumbing calls are simpler. If your address is near the square or on the streets that lead into it, this page is for you.

Older housing near the square, as the state survey describes it

The survey names several streets close to the center. Elm Street, South Street and Crescent Street have Greek Revival cottages, Plymouth Street has two-family Queen Anne and Colonial Revival houses, and Main Street north of the square has Italianate double houses. Birch Street was described as a modest but stylish suburb.

That is a record of how the buildings look, not a record of what is inside the walls. Plumbing is often replaced piece by piece over a century. A house that is old on the outside can have a newer water heater, a newer supply line and an old drain, in any combination.

Sewer and the area around the center

The town's downtown plan calls the residential area around the central business district largely sewered. Properties in that area are more likely to connect to a municipal line, though you should confirm your own address. The town's Sewer Division sits within the Department of Public Works. If a backup affects only your building, the blockage is usually on your side of the connection.

If it affects the street, call the town. The Water and Sewer office is at the Highway Barn, 151 High Street, and the Police Department answers for the town after hours. We can help you work out which situation you are in before you call anyone.

Permits and who to ask in Bridgewater

According to the town's Building Department page, Bridgewater requires a separate plumbing permit for plumbing work tied to a building project, with rough and final inspections. The Municipal Office Building is at 66 Central Square. Ask the department what applies before a water heater swap, a sewer repair or a bathroom remodel.
