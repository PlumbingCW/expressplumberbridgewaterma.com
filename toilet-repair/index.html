Emergency Toilet Repair in Bridgewater, MA

An overflowing or leaking toilet needs attention right away. We stop the water, fix the cause and replace the toilet when a repair will not hold.

Call (508) 389-4045

Overflowing toilet: stop the water first

A toilet that is overflowing is an emergency toilet repair call in Bridgewater, MA, and we answer 24/7. Turn the valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop the supply. If the valve is stuck, lift the float in the tank to stop the fill or close the main water valve.

Do not flush again. Move rugs away and put towels around the base. If the toilet backs up and other drains gurgle, the blockage is past the toilet. Ask about timing when you call, including after-hours visits.

Common toilet problems

  • ✓

    Running toilet: a worn flapper or a fill valve that does not shut off.

  • ✓

    Leak at the base: a failed wax ring or loose closet bolts.

  • ✓

    Weak flush: mineral build-up in the rim jets, a clogged siphon or a low water level.

  • ✓

    Rocking toilet: loose bolts or a damaged flange.

  • ✓

    Leak at the supply connection: a worn supply line or valve.

Many fixes are small parts. A leak at the floor often means the wax ring and flange need attention, and a damaged floor may need repair.

Leaks at the base and the flange

A toilet sits on a flange and a wax ring seals the joint. If the toilet rocks or the ring fails, water leaks onto the floor, often with a slow, hidden soak. We pull the toilet, inspect the flange and the subfloor, replace the ring and reset the toilet.

If the flange is cracked or sits too low, a repair ring or a new flange fixes it. A soft floor is a sign the leak has been going on, and we show you before we reset the toilet. A leak that keeps coming back can also be a drain line problem. See leak detection if the water source is unclear.

Running toilets and iron-stained tanks

A running toilet wastes water in a steady trickle you do not hear. Replacing the flapper and fill valve is quick and inexpensive. Mineral and iron deposits can foul a flapper or the fill valve seat. The state's source water assessment lists iron treatment at the Bridgewater Water Department's wells, so rust-colored stains in a tank can happen when iron reaches the home.

The town's water page says discoloration from iron is generally harmless and often clears after running cold water for a few minutes. If it persists, contact the Water Department. Stains in the tank are a cosmetic issue, but they can wear parts faster.

Code and sources for this work

Federal law sets a maximum of 1.6 gallons per flush for toilets sold in the United States, and the EPA WaterSense label marks models at 1.28 gallons or less. That standard is why a replacement often uses less water than the old bowl.
