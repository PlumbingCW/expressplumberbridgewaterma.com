Tankless water heater installation and service in Bridgewater

A tankless water heater in Bridgewater, MA heats water as it flows through a heat exchanger, so there is no tank to fill and no standby loss. That is the draw. The catch is that the unit needs enough gas flow or electric capacity, a vent rated for it and routine flushing to keep the exchanger clear.

We install new units, service existing ones and flush them. Some plumbers will replace a tankless unit but will not service one. We do both.

Is tankless right for your house

  • ✓

    The gas line is large enough for the unit's burner, or the electrical panel can carry an electric model.

  • ✓

    The vent can be run to the outside in a stainless or approved material.

  • ✓

    You are ready for a higher upfront cost in exchange for endless hot water at a steady temperature.

  • ✓

    You will schedule a flush so scale does not build up.

If the gas line or panel would need major work, a standard tank may be the better fit. We tell you before you buy, and our water heater installation page covers tank options.

Flushing and descaling

Minerals in the water coat the heat exchanger over time. The coating makes the unit work harder, trips error codes and shortens its life. A flush circulates a descaling solution, usually white vinegar or a manufacturer-approved cleaner, through the exchanger with a small pump and hoses.

How often depends on your water. Bridgewater's water comes from groundwater wells, and the town publishes an annual water quality report, so ask the Water Department for the current figures or test your tap. Harder water means more frequent flushing. If you have not flushed the unit in a few years, schedule one before an error code forces the issue.

Codes vary by brand, and we read the unit's manual and the displayed code before touching anything.

Stainless vent pipe and gas capacity

Many tankless units need stainless steel or an approved vent material, and the vent length and elbows are limited by the manufacturer. A condensing unit also needs a drain for the acidic condensate. We check the vent route before we quote, since a long run through a finished wall changes the price.

A tankless burner uses a lot of gas at once, so the meter, the pipe size and the run from the meter all matter. We size the gas line to the unit. If the existing line is too small, we tell you what it takes to upgrade.

Code and sources for this work

Tankless venting, gas sizing and condensate drains must match the maker's installation manual and the state codes, 248 CMR 10.00 and 248 CMR 5.00. Those documents are the standard we install to.
