Plumbing installation for remodels and additions

Plumbing installation in Bridgewater, MA covers the work that goes in before the walls are closed and the fixtures are set. A new bathroom, a finished basement, a kitchen remodel or a laundry move all need supply lines, drains, vents and shutoff valves in the right places.

We work with homeowners and contractors. Tell us what the room will look like and where the fixtures go, and we will tell you what the plumbing takes. A plan at the start avoids tearing out a wall later.

From rough-in to final

  1. We review the plans and the site, then give you a clear price before work starts.

  2. We rough in the supply, drain and vent lines before the walls are closed.

  3. The town's plumbing inspector checks the rough work before it is covered.

  4. We set the fixtures, connect them and test the system.

  5. A final inspection closes the permit.

For a single-family home, Bridgewater's Building Department lists a rough plumbing inspection before the rough frame inspection and a final inspection that includes the final plumbing.

Permits and inspections in Bridgewater

Bridgewater's Building Department says plumbing or gas work tied to a building project needs a separate permit alongside the building permit. The town uses an electronic permit system, and its page says a permit card must be in hand before work begins, with a triple permit fee penalty for failing to comply.

Massachusetts licenses plumbers, and the state's Uniform Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10, governs the work. We handle the plumbing permit and the inspections for our jobs. Ask any plumber for the permit and check their licence on the state's verification page.

Water softeners and filtration

Many homeowners ask about water softeners and filters. The first step is to know your water. Bridgewater's public water comes from groundwater wells, and the Water Department publishes an annual water quality report. Ask the department for it, or test your tap, before you buy a system.

A softener treats hardness, and a filter targets specific contaminants. Neither is a cure-all, and the right choice depends on what a test finds. We install and connect systems, run the drain and bypass and set up the plumbing. We do not recommend a system before you know what is in your water.

Planning the plumbing before the walls close

The cheapest time to change a plumbing plan is before the framing is covered. Decide where the toilet, the vanity and the shower sit, and confirm where the drain and vent can run through the joists. Buy the fixtures early so the rough-in measurements come from the real parts, not a catalog guess.
