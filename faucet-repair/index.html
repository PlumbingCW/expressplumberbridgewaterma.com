Water spraying or a valve that will not close

A burst supply line or a faucet that will not shut off is an emergency faucet repair in Bridgewater, MA, and we answer 24/7. Close the angle stop under the sink by turning it clockwise. If it will not turn or leaks, shut off the main water valve and call us.

Put a bucket under the leak and wipe up any water near the cabinet. We ask about timing when you call and ask which fixture is involved, how old it is and whether the shutoff worked.

Many faucets are repaired with a new cartridge or washer. A faucet with a corroded body is cheaper to replace than to repair.

Why shutoff valves matter

The small valves under sinks and behind toilets are called angle stops. They are the quickest way to stop a leak, and they often seize if they are never turned. A valve that does not close or drips from the stem needs replacing, and a plumber should do it because replacing a valve touches the supply line.

Massachusetts's plumbing guidance says minor repairs, such as a leaky faucet, do not need a permit, while replacing or relocating a faucet, valve or piping that affects public health may. Ask the Bridgewater Building Department how a valve replacement is handled. We tell you if a permit step applies.

Tub, shower and bathtub faucets

A tub that drips from the spout, a shower that runs hot and cold or a handle that will not shut off often has a worn cartridge or valve stem. Replacing the cartridge is quick. If the valve is old and corroded, replacing the whole valve is a bigger job that opens the wall.

A shower or tub drain that is slow is usually a clog, not a faucet issue. See drain cleaning. If water is staining a ceiling below a bathroom, leak detection finds where it comes from.

Aerators and iron in Bridgewater water

The state's source water assessment lists iron treatment at the Bridgewater Water Department's wells. Iron and sediment can clog faucet aerators and make water flow weak at one tap. Unscrewing the aerator and rinsing it often restores flow.

The town says iron discoloration is generally harmless and clears after running cold water for a few minutes, especially after hydrant flushing or main work. If a faucet stays weak after the aerator is cleaned, the cartridge or the supply line is the next place to look.

Code and sources for this work

Faucets sold in the United States are limited by federal standard to 2.2 gallons per minute at 60 psi. According to the EPA, a steady drip can waste thousands of gallons a year, so a small repair pays back.
